# Decision register: the brief's 13 decisions

Each entry gives the answer, the reason, the evidence and what it does not cover. Evidence marked E1 to E11 is in
[decision-evidence.md](verification/decision-evidence.md), regenerated by `python scripts/submission_evidence.py`.
The dated engineering log (D1 onward) is [decision-register.md](decision-register.md). Policy values marked with a Q number
are our defaults and are **not approved by operations** ([operations-questions.md](operations-questions.md)).

## Understand

### 1. What must be collected before useful options are shown?
**Answer.** Which shipment, and either an arrival time, a delay length, or for a breakdown the repair time **and** the drive
time afterwards. Optional: earliest or latest acceptable time. Everything else is looked up.
**Reason.** The backend already holds the load, truck, appointment and dock rules. Asking for them wastes the driver's time.
**Evidence.** E1: three example inputs including seed chats THR001 and THR003, and the two questions the service asks.
**Limit.** The seed agent asks whether "one hour" means a new arrival time or more waiting. Our rule does not: it reads it
as one hour after the planned ETA.

### 2. How does a conversation link to the right driver, shipment and appointment?
**Answer.** Driver from the verified channel (Telegram contact binding, or a signed phone request), never from message text.
Shipment named in the conversation and checked against that driver on every tool call. Appointment looked up from the
shipment's current row.
**Reason.** Identity must not be something a message can claim. One driver can hold two loads.
**Evidence.** E2: message-to-rows trace for THR010, where DRV004 has two open loads and names neither. Scenario check 9
refuses another driver's load.
**Limit.** Which of a driver's own two loads is meant is chosen by the model, guided by its instructions to ask. Code
checks ownership, not that the question was asked.

### 3. How is the revised ETA set, and how sure are we?
**Answer.** "N minutes late" = planned ETA + N. A stated time is used as stated. A repair time alone is not an ETA: the
drive time is needed. A gate-in contradicts a delay report and goes to a person. Every ETA carries its source, baseline
and confidence; a GPS estimate that disagrees lowers the confidence.
**Reason.** A repair time is not an arrival time, and a recorded gate-in is stronger evidence than a message.
**Evidence.** E3 applies the rule to two messages. Scenario check 2.
**Limit.** Confidence is a label (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW), not a measured accuracy. No ETA buffer is applied (Q3).

## Decide

### 4. What makes a slot feasible for this shipment?
**Answer.** All of: in the future and reachable by the ETA; slot open and unoccupied; dock active with no event; inside
facility hours and the last-start time; dock type, refrigeration and weight fit; unload fits the slot; within the driver's
time limits; labour and equipment available; consignee commitment known and met. Missing labour or consignee data gives
"cannot confirm", never "assume available".
**Reason.** An empty row in the slot table is not capacity.
**Evidence.** E4 runs the check on SHP1006, SHP1014 and SHP1015, with and without test labour data, with rejection counts.
Code: `src/setuhaul/feasibility.py`.
**Limit.** The supplied data has no labour or consignee rows, so the positive results rely on `TEST_FIXTURE` rows.

### 5. What does "available" mean while another driver is deciding?
**Answer.** A slot is available when no appointment in HELD, PENDING_CONFIRMATION, CONFIRMED or IN_PROGRESS uses it. A
live hold makes it unavailable to everyone else at once. A shown option reserves nothing.
**Reason.** Two readers can see one free slot; only the write can decide. Holds live in the same table and unique index as
bookings, so a planner or another tool collides with them too.
**Evidence.** `ux_active_appointment_per_slot` (migration 002); E7; [concurrency-proof.md](verification/concurrency-proof.md).
**Limit.** A driver can be shown a slot that is gone a moment later; the hold attempt then fails safely.

## Commit

### 6. When does an option become a hold, request or confirmed booking?
**Answer.** The service shows and immediately **holds** the first feasible slot for 3 minutes; the driver's Yes confirms it.

| State | Moved by | Driver sees |
|---|---|---|
| Shown | search | an offer, nothing reserved |
| Held | service, after a search | "I am holding ... It is not booked yet. Reply Yes to confirm, or No for the next slot." |
| Confirmed | driver's Yes, one transaction | "Your appointment is confirmed" with a reference |
| Released or expired | driver's No, timer, or position change | "Nothing is booked; your original appointment stands" |
| Requested (partner warehouse) | service emails the warehouse | "This is only a request until they reply" |
| Cancelled | driver, within policy | "Your appointment has been cancelled" |

**Reason.** Showing is not reserving, and reserving is not confirming. The old appointment ends only inside the
transaction that confirms the new one.
**Evidence.** Scenario checks 3, 10 and 11; D24, D29, D93.
**Limit.** The 3-minute lifetime is untested with real drivers (Q1).

### 7. How are simultaneous requests ordered when capacity runs short?
**Answer.** **First successful hold commit wins**, after hard eligibility checks. Priority is advisory and never takes a
held slot.
**Reason.** It is predictable, cannot take back a promise already made, and needs no invented priority weights.
**Evidence.** E7: SHP1006 (HIGH) and SHP1012 (NORMAL) compete in both orders; the first commit wins each time. Scenario
check 6.
**Trade-off.** A critical load can lose to an earlier normal one. Operations has not approved this (Q4, Q5).

### 8. (Optional) When does the facility schedule recalculate?
**Not attempted.** There is no facility-wide scheduling tool. Searches are recalculated per request, and a new truck
position re-checks that driver's hold. Nothing re-sequences other trucks.

### 9. How are stale options, cancellations, duplicate messages and retries handled?
**Answer.** Stored options are compared with live state before use. Every write is re-checked inside its transaction.
Confirm and cancel are idempotent per driver, thread, message and action. A duplicate inbound message returns the stored
reply without calling the model. Cancellation is automatic only 120 minutes or more ahead with a known, penalty-free
commitment (Q6).
**Reason.** Chat channels repeat messages and the schedule changes between turns.
**Evidence.** E9 replays a stale option and a retried confirmation; the named tests replay duplicates and cancellation.
**Limit.** The channel integration has not been exercised with real retries at volume.

## Fail safely

### 10. What happens when no feasible slot exists?
**Answer.** Nothing is promised, the booking is not changed, and a case is opened with an owner and a lifecycle.
**Reason.** An invented answer is worse than a hand-off.
**Evidence.** E10: the message and the stored case. Scenario: DRV014 / SHP1014, whose 75-minute unload fits no 60-minute
slot.
**Limit.** No response time is promised (Q16).

### 11. What is said when the preferred slot is not granted?
**Answer.** Plain statement of what happened, what is unchanged, and what can be done next.
**Reason.** A refusal without a reason costs trust.
**Evidence.** E11: lapsed hold, unreachable slot and service outage messages, word for word.
**Limit.** When another driver takes a slot first, the service holds the next feasible one and **does not tell the driver
why the earlier time was not offered**. This is a gap. Hindi and Hinglish wording is unreviewed.

### 12. Which decisions need human approval or takeover?

| Trigger | Case or approval |
|---|---|
| No feasible slot | `NO_SLOT` |
| Delay report contradicts gate-in | `CONTRADICTORY_DATA` |
| Labour or consignee data missing | `INCOMPLETE_FEASIBILITY_DATA` |
| Slot breaks a consignee deadline with a penalty | `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` |
| Appointment already in progress | `POLICY_CONFLICT` |
| Cancellation under 120 minutes ahead | `CANCELLATION_CUTOFF` |
| Penalty or unknown customer commitment on cancel | `CUSTOMER_COMMITMENT`, `CUSTOMER_COMMITMENT_UNKNOWN` |
| Safety, regulated load, driver asks for a person | `SAFETY`, `REGULATED_LOAD`, `DRIVER_REQUESTED` |
| Database or model failure, loop limit, unsupported language | `DATABASE_UNAVAILABLE`, `MODEL_UNAVAILABLE`, `MODEL_LOOP_LIMIT`, `LANGUAGE_UNSUPPORTED` |
| Driver wants another facility | destination warehouse or admin approves |
| Load bound for a partner warehouse | booked only after their approval reply |

**Reason.** Safety, money, disputes and missing evidence carry risk a model should not own.
**Evidence.** `src/setuhaul/escalation.py`; scenario checks 14 to 18; D28, D69, D93.
**Limit.** The safety and regulated-load triggers depend on the model recognising them. No rule in code detects them, and
this has not been measured with a real model.

## Prove

### 13. How is it proved that capacity was not double-booked?
**Answer.** Competing requests are released together from separate connections, threads and processes, then read-only
invariant queries check the database. The database's partial unique index is the backstop behind the application checks.
**Reason.** The claim is only worth what a test that can fail shows.
**Evidence.** [concurrency-proof.md](verification/concurrency-proof.md): 20 rounds of 10 threads and 20 rounds of
6 processes on one slot, one winner every round, 0 invariant violations. Planted-fault tests show the checker notices a
broken protection (D33, D35). Run: `python scripts/run_integrity_proof.py`.
**Limit.** SQLite on one machine. Not shown under sustained load, across machines or on PostgreSQL.
